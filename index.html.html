Clay vs. Natural Cat Litter: Which Is Better?

There isn't a universal winner. Your cat's preferences and your household's needs matter more than the label on the bag.

Clay Litter

Advantages:

  • Many clumping options
  • Widely available
  • Strong odor-control choices
  • Available at different price points

Potential drawbacks:

  • Can be heavy
  • Some formulas can track
  • Dust levels vary

Natural Litter

Advantages:

  • Often plant-based
  • Many lightweight options
  • Some formulas focus on low tracking and dust

Potential drawbacks:

  • Different texture
  • Cats may need time to adjust
  • Can cost more

Don't Judge Cat Litter by Bag Price Alone

Litter A costs less but forms weak clumps. Each scoop removes some clean litter too.

Litter B costs more but forms tighter clumps. You remove less clean litter during each cleaning.

Which is actually cheaper? You can't know from shelf price alone.

Purchase price + wasted litter + replacement frequency + cleaning effort

That's the real cost of using the product.

How to Get Better Odor Control From Any Cat Litter

Even the best cat litter won't eliminate odor if the litter box isn't maintained properly.

Scoop regularly

Removing waste promptly can reduce odor buildup.

Don't rely entirely on fragrance

Fragrance can change how a room smells without addressing the source.

Keep enough litter in the box

Adequate depth can help urine form removable clumps.

Clean the box regularly

Even good litter can't prevent residue from accumulating indefinitely.

Is natural cat litter better than clay?

Not necessarily. Consider texture, odor control, tracking, dust, clumping, cat acceptance, and budget.

Is clumping cat litter better?

Clumping litter can make daily cleanup easier because you can remove urine and waste without replacing all the litter.

How often should cat litter be scooped?

Litter boxes should be scooped regularly to remove waste and reduce odor. More frequently used boxes generally need more frequent attention.
